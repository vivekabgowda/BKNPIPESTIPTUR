Manufactured as per IS 1678

IS 1678 is the Bureau of Indian Standards specification that governs prestressed concrete poles for overhead power, traction and telecommunication lines. It lays down how the poles must be designed and dimensioned, the quality of concrete and prestressing steel to be used, and the strength the finished pole must achieve. Electricity boards and their contractors specify IS 1678 poles in practically every distribution tender, which is why we manufacture all our 8m, 9m and 11m poles to this standard.

To be clear about what we claim: our poles are manufactured as per IS 1678 specifications. We follow the standard's requirements for materials, dimensions and workmanship, and we back that up with strict in-house quality control described further below.

What Does "Prestressed" Actually Mean?

PSC stands for prestressed cement concrete, and the idea is simpler than it sounds.

Concrete is very strong when you squeeze it (compression) but weak when you stretch it (tension). An electric pole standing in the wind, carrying the pull of conductors, is constantly being bent — and bending stretches one face of the pole. Plain concrete would crack there.

In a prestressed pole, high-tensile steel wires are stretched tight inside the mould before the concrete is poured. Once the concrete hardens and grips the wires, the wires are released. They try to spring back to their original length, and in doing so they squeeze the entire concrete pole into permanent compression — like a row of books held firmly between two hands.

Now, when wind or conductor load bends the pole, the bending first has to overcome that built-in squeeze before the concrete ever feels any stretch. The practical result:

  • The pole resists far higher bending loads than an ordinary reinforced pole of the same size.
  • Hairline cracking is suppressed, so rainwater cannot reach the steel and rust it from inside.
  • The pole can be made slimmer and lighter for the same strength, which lowers material and transport cost.

Quality Control — What We Actually Do

We will not dress this up with certificates we do not hold. What we do maintain, on every batch of poles, is disciplined in-house quality control:

  • Controlled batching: cement, aggregates and water are proportioned by measure, not by guesswork, so concrete strength stays consistent from pole to pole.
  • Proper tensioning: prestressing wires are stressed to the designed level before casting — the single most important step in a PSC pole.
  • Water curing: poles are cured thoroughly so the concrete gains its full design strength before dispatch.
  • Dimensional & visual inspection: every pole is checked for length, cross-section, straightness, hole positions and surface finish before it leaves the yard.

For reference, IS 1678 itself prescribes strength requirements — including a transverse strength test in which a pole is loaded sideways to verify it meets its specified load — and dimensional tolerances that finished poles must satisfy. Our manufacturing is planned around those requirements, and a detailed spec sheet for each pole length is available on request. You are welcome to visit the Kunigal unit and inspect stock before ordering — most of our institutional buyers do.
